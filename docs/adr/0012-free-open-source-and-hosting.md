# ADR 0012: Keep MIT software free initially and gate any hosted-fee proposal

## Context

The requested initial product is free/open source, with possible fees later if useful results justify a service. A hoped-for 90% score has no defined target, denominator or evidence and cannot trigger commercialization.

## Decision proposed

Record existing MIT licensing and free initial access. Propose retaining reproducible code/permitted research and a distinct future hosted-service business proposal. Before any billing scope, review actual user value, costs, support, data entitlements, privacy/offboarding, evidence-backed claims and explicit product authorization.

## Alternatives

A paywall now exceeds scope. Treating all data as open because code is MIT violates independent rights. Automatically charging after a headline accuracy threshold substitutes a metric for product/economic review.

## Consequences and current limits

No billing or license enforcement is introduced. Private user data remains private; prior MIT grants are not withdrawn by a future service. Free public hosting still requires provider rights and operational readiness.

## Validation and delivery

Check contribution/setup instructions, license notice, absence of a new fee flow and traceability of claims. A future proposal needs cost evidence, support/recovery/export plan and permission matrix before implementation.

## Decisions still required

Pricing, customer terms, support staffing, budgets, approved data plans and paid offering scope are explicitly undecided. #198 is the decision-review issue; #184/#196/#188 remain enabling gates, not billing tasks.
